What to Fix Before Listing Your Tampa Bay Home, Room by Room

How I walk a home before it lists, from the street view to the small fixes buyers notice.

Nachelle Kershner November 2, 2026 Tampa Bay, FL 6 min read

When I walk through a home before it goes on the market, I try to see it the way a buyer will see it: first on a phone screen, then from the curb, then at the front door. Most of what makes a buyer pause is not a big repair. It is a stained caulk line, a burned-out bulb, a door that will not latch, or a wall color that no longer feels fresh.

Online photos matter more than most sellers expect. Redfin reports that for 81 percent of buyers, listing photos are one of the most valuable parts of searching for a home online. That means a buyer forms an opinion before anyone steps inside. So I think about the home in the order buyers meet it: outside first, then the living areas, then the rooms that get the most scrutiny.

Here is how I walk a Tampa Bay home, room by room.

Outside: the street view and the front door

Buyers judge the house from the street long before the showing starts. Fading, peeling or stained exterior paint tells them maintenance was put off, and it is the first thing a listing photo shows. In Florida, humidity lets mold, mildew and algae grow on painted surfaces, so any exterior painting should start with a thorough cleaning of the surface, not just a new coat on top.

I look at the paint, the front door, the trim, the driveway and the walkways. Pressure washing the house, driveway and sidewalks is often one of the quickest changes, and then exterior painting and curb appeal work can build on a clean surface. You can see what that includes on my services page.

The living room: where staging matters most

If I could only get one room right, it would be the living room. In the National Association of REALTORS 2025 Profile of Home Staging, the living room ranked as the most important room to stage (37 percent), followed by the primary bedroom (34 percent) and the kitchen (23 percent). The same report found that 83 percent of buyers' agents said staging makes it easier for a buyer to picture the property as their future home.

That does not mean filling a room with furniture. It means clear floors, a layout that shows the size of the room, fewer personal items, and walls that feel clean and neutral. If the walls are scuffed or the color is very personal, a fresh coat of paint is usually the first thing I recommend. You can read more about my painting and staging work on my services page.

The kitchen: clean beats remodel

I rarely suggest a kitchen remodel just to list a home. A true deep clean makes a bigger difference than most sellers expect. Redfin notes that sellers often overlook the deep clean because they stop noticing fingerprints, dusty vents and stained grout that buyers see right away. I would also check the small things: cabinet hardware that has come loose, dripping faucets, and bulbs that have burned out.

If you are thinking about a bigger kitchen project, talk with your listing agent first about whether it makes sense for your price range and neighborhood.

Bathrooms: grout and fresh paint

Bathrooms are small, so every flaw shows. Old caulk, stained grout, a running toilet or a faded wall can make the whole room feel neglected. A good cleaning and a clean coat of paint often change how the room photographs and feels.

The primary bedroom: calm and neutral

Buyers want to picture themselves resting here. Very bold wall colors, crowded furniture and piles of personal items work against that. I look for a calm, neutral wall color, a clear path through the room, and simple bedding. If the walls are bright or dark, repainting before photos can change the whole feel of the room.

The small fixes that signal care

Before listing, walk through with fresh eyes and fix the inexpensive things that make a house look neglected: damaged trim, loose hardware, dripping faucets, burned-out bulbs, and doors that do not close properly. Redfin recommends fixing inexpensive issues like these before listing. None of them is expensive, but together they tell a buyer the home has been looked after.

What I would not spend money on first

Not every project is worth doing before you list. Big remodels, new flooring throughout, or landscaping overhauls are better discussed with your listing agent first, since they know what buyers in your price range expect. I focus on the things that make a home look clean, cared for and move-in ready, because those are usually the best use of a seller's money and time.

Frequently asked questions

How early should I start preparing my home to sell?

As early as you can, and before listing photos are booked. The right timeline depends on the work, so I will give you one at your free on-site estimate.

Do I still need staging if I live in the home?

It can help. The NAR report found that 83 percent of buyers' agents said staging helps buyers visualize the property as their future home. If you still live in the home, decluttering and rearranging what you already own is a good first step.

Is a deep clean really worth it?

Redfin points to deep cleaning as one of the things sellers overlook most often. Buyers notice fingerprints, dusty vents and stained grout immediately.

Statistics above come from surveys of real estate agents and buyers by the National Association of REALTORS and Redfin. They describe what agents and buyers reported, not the results of any one sale.
